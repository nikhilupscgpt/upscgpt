# Climatology: Structure of the Atmosphere and Insolation

## 1. Structure of the Atmosphere
The atmosphere is structured into several concentric layers based on temperature variations:

- **Troposphere**: The lowest layer of the atmosphere. It extends up to 8 km at the poles and 18 km at the equator. Temperature decreases with height at a normal lapse rate of 6.5°C per kilometer. All weather phenomena (clouds, rain, storms) occur in this layer.
- **Stratosphere**: Extends up to 50 km. Temperature increases with height due to the absorption of ultraviolet radiation by the ozone layer (ozonosphere), located between 15 km and 35 km. This layer is free from clouds and weather disturbances, making it ideal for flying jet aircraft.
- **Mesosphere**: Extends up to 80 km. Temperature decreases with height, reaching the lowest temperatures in the atmosphere (around -100°C) at the mesopause.
- **Thermosphere**: Extends from 80 km to 400 km. Contains the Ionosphere, which reflects radio waves back to Earth. Temperature increases rapidly with height.
- **Exosphere**: The outermost zone, merging gradually into outer space.

## 2. Insolation and Heat Budget
Insolation refers to the incoming solar radiation received by the Earth.
- **Factors affecting Insolation**:
  1. Angle of inclination of the sun's rays.
  2. Length of the day.
  3. Distance between Earth and Sun (Aphelion and Perihelion).
  4. Atmospheric transparency.

- **Heat Budget of the Earth**:
  Out of 100 units of incoming solar radiation:
  - **35 units** are reflected back to space before reaching the surface (albedo of the Earth):
    - 27 units reflected by clouds.
    - 6 units scattered by dust and gas.
    - 2 units reflected by snow/ice covers.
  - **65 units** are absorbed:
    - 14 units absorbed by the atmosphere.
    - 51 units absorbed by the Earth's surface (which is later radiated back as longwave terrestrial radiation).

## 3. Atmospheric Circulation and Wind Systems
Global winds are driven by pressure belts and the Coriolis force:
- **Hadley Cell**: Warm air rises at the equator, moves poleward, and sinks at around 30° latitude (Subtropical High).
- **Ferrel Cell**: Mid-latitude circulation where air flows poleward and upward at 60° latitude (Subpolar Low).
- **Polar Cell**: Cold air sinks at the poles and moves toward subpolar latitudes.

- **Primary Winds**:
  - **Trade Winds**: Blow from Subtropical Highs toward the Equatorial Low.
  - **Westerlies**: Blow from Subtropical Highs toward Subpolar Lows.
  - **Polar Easterlies**: Blow from Polar Highs toward Subpolar Lows.
